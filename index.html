08 / Defend the reasoning

Show the evidence.
Know the limits.

Why is this supply chain management rather than operations?

We compare supplier relationships, ownership, inventory policy and distribution across organisations. We do not explain grilling, staffing or the sequence of kitchen tasks.

What does the evidence not establish?

A fixed delivery radius, daily delivery to every In-N-Out store, unit-cost rankings or superior food safety. The fresh-versus-frozen distinction does not by itself explain network ownership.

Can In-N-Out expand?

Yes. Its December 2025 Tennessee opening release is evidence of expansion. The claim is that freshness creates coordination constraints, not that growth is impossible or requires a new facility in every region.

Burger King’s U.S. network includes Restaurant Services, Inc., or RSI. Burger King sets standards and approves suppliers, while RSI coordinates purchasing and distribution arrangements with approved partners. This is governance across separate firms. Pooling demand can support purchasing negotiations, while common specifications help align the network. Coordination still depends on information and agreements between the participants. That is the mechanism we want to explain. Our evidence establishes the cooperative’s role. It does not establish a particular beef delivery frequency or prove that all Burger King patties are frozen. The comparison therefore concerns coordination, rather than an unsupported claim about the product.

Freezing can extend the holding window between production and sale. That may give a planner more flexibility when replenishment is delayed, provided usable stock is available. Jimena Navarro · Disruption lab and Quarter Pounder test

  • Ask the audience to choose ONE disruption.
  • Explain the mechanism and its limits.
  • Run the freshness vote before revealing the answer.

On screen: Use only the selected scenario paragraph. Allow 15 seconds for the choice, 30 for that scenario, 20 for the vote and 35 for the reveal. Advance from the lab to Challenge the claim.

Suggested wording to say aloud

Let’s test the mechanism. Choose a delivery delay, a facility outage or expansion into a new region. We will discuss one scenario. Delivery delay: a fresh line has less room to respond through long inventory holding. Frozen stock may help, but only where usable stock is available. Ownership alone does not remove transport risk. Facility outage: internal control does not create spare capacity. Both owned and external networks need qualified capacity. We cannot assume a backup plant exists. Regional expansion: new restaurants need viable facility coverage and replenishment. A never-frozen policy tightens those constraints. In-N-Out’s Tennessee expansion shows growth is possible. Now, does fresh beef require owning patty production? Vote before I reveal the answer. No. McDonald’s fresh Quarter Pounder line used its supply-chain partners. Freshness can work through different governance arrangements when the network fits the promise.
